> **Today there are no new words: there's a distinction.** You put together two sentences you already know separately and find out they don't mean the same thing. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - tell **Э́то моя́ су́мка** apart from **У меня́ есть су́мка**;
> - pick the right sentence for what you actually mean;
> - switch from one to the other by changing only the opening.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

The two constructions from memory, out loud:

> Э́то моя́ су́мка. · У меня́ есть су́мка.

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

Review the unstressed **о** in **моя́** and **него́**: spot it before you
read today's sentences aloud.

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat, switching pace:*

> Э́то моя́ су́мка. — У меня́ есть су́мка.
> Э́то её ла́мпа. — У неё есть ла́мпа.

## 3. New (8–18 min)

**No new grammar today: the text below combines Э́то + possessive**
(unit 7) **and у + есть** (unit 6), which until now lived in separate
lessons.

> — Чья э́то су́мка?
> *Whose bag is this?*
> — Э́то моя́ су́мка.
> *This bag is mine.*
> — А у тебя́ есть су́мка?
> *And do you have a bag?*
> — Да, у меня́ есть су́мка. Вот она́.
> *Yes, I have a bag. Here it is.*

**Э́то моя́ су́мка identifies a specific bag**: the one in front of both
of you, pointed at with **э́то**. **У меня́ есть су́мка states a fact**:
that you have one, without pointing at any particular bag. The second
sentence doesn't identify anything; it just says it exists.

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**English also tells "this is my bag" apart from "I have a bag," but
both start the same way, with the verb up front.** In Russian, what
changes is the WHOLE OPENING, not just the verb:

| You mean | Say |
|---|---|
| This table is mine (pointing at it). | Э́то мой стол. |
| I have a table (none is in front of you). | У меня́ есть стол. |

Mix up the two openings and you can end up pointing at something you
don't actually have, or claiming to have something you're actually
pointing at.

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read the dialogue from "New" aloud, both voices.*

### Б. Pointing or stating: which is it?

*Does the sentence point at something in front of you, or just say it
exists?*

1. Э́то моя́ ла́мпа. → а) points · б) states
2. У меня́ есть ла́мпа. → а) points · б) states
3. Чьи э́то ключи́? → а) points · б) states
4. У тебя́ есть ключи́? → а) points · б) states

### В. Переведи́те.

*Translate into English.*

1. Э́то её су́мка.
2. У него́ есть су́мка.

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**One object, two sentences.** Pick an object that's really near you.
First, point at it and say **Э́то мой/моя́…**; then, without pointing,
say **У меня́ есть…**. Notice the difference as you say them out loud.

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **Now I know:** **Э́то моя́ су́мка** points at something specific and **У меня́ есть су́мка** just states that it exists, and switching between them only changes the opening.
