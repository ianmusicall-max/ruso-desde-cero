> **Today** you read a moving-day scene with the labels all mixed up, and learn how Russians ask neighbours for help instead of hiring a van.

## 1. Before you read (0–5 min)

In many countries, moving house means hiring a van and sometimes movers. In
Russia, it's common to do it with neighbours and friends with a car instead
—and you thank them with food, not money. Think: how did you move house
last time? Write one line.

## 2. Text (5–18 min)

Camila has just arrived with boxes. The labels got mixed up on the way, and
Tom is trying to help her find each thing.

> Том: Где твой стол?
> Ками́ла: Вот он, здесь, в коро́бке.
> Том: А где твои́ часы́?
> Ками́ла: Они́ там, в друго́й коро́бке.
> Том: А де́ньги?
> Ками́ла: У меня́ в су́мке. У меня́ есть де́ньги, не беспоко́йся.
> Том: Хорошо́. А у тебя́ есть крова́ть?
> Ками́ла: Нет, пока́ нет. То́лько матра́с.

Read it again aloud, switching voices between Tom and Camila.

## 3. Words (18–23 min)

| Russian | English |
|---|---|
| коро́бка | box |
| друго́й | another |
| не беспоко́йся | don't worry |
| матра́с | mattress |

## 4. Task (23–29 min)

**True or false.**

1. Camila's watch is in the same box as the table.
2. Camila has the money in her bag.
3. Camila already has a bed.

Then, out loud, say which three things of yours you'd pack in each box if
you were moving tomorrow.

## 5. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **You now know:** that moving house in Russia is often a job for neighbours and friends, not a company, and that **не беспоко́йся** calms someone who's nervous about a move.

Tomorrow the unit's second week starts: he and she, politely.

## Answers

1. False (they're in different boxes). · 2. True. · 3. False (she only has
a mattress, for now).
