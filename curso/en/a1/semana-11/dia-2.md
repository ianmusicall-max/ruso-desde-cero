> **Today** you learn three Russian words that have no singular at all: they're always "them". By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - say **часы́**, **очки́** and **де́ньги**, and use them with **они́**;
> - tell when **У меня́ есть** takes **он/она́/оно́** or **они́**;
> - name three more objects from your room.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

From yesterday, the six objects and their question:

> У тебя́ есть стол? Стул? Крова́ть? Шкаф? Письмо́? Фотогра́фия?

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

Today's three words carry the stress on the last syllable, the one with
**ы́** or **и́**:

| Word | Say it |
|---|---|
| часы́ | chi-**SY** |
| очки́ | ach-**KEE** |
| де́ньги | **DYEN'**-gee |

**Де́ньги** is the exception: there the stress sits on the first syllable,
and the **н** is soft, tongue against the roof of the mouth, as in
**день**.

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat:*

> часы́ — очки́ — де́ньги
> зе́ркало — моне́та — фотоаппара́т

## 3. New (8–18 min)

**Three things Russian has no singular for.** A watch, glasses, money: all
three are always said in the plural, even for one single object, and so
they always take **они́**:

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| часы́ | chi-**SY** | watch, clock |
| очки́ | ach-**KEE** | glasses |
| де́ньги | **DYEN'**-gee | money |

> — У тебя́ есть часы́?
> *Have you got a watch?*
> — Да, вот они́.
> *Yes, here it is.* (literally: "here they are")

Notice: you don't say **он** even for a single watch. **Часы́** always
takes **они́**, just like English "the scissors" is always plural even for
one single tool.

And three more objects, these with a normal singular:

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| зе́ркало | **ZYER**-ka-la | mirror |
| моне́та | ma-**NYE**-ta | coin |
| фотоаппара́т | fo-ta-a-pa-**RAT** | camera |

> — У меня́ есть зе́ркало и фотоаппара́т.
> *I've got a mirror and a camera.*
> — А у меня́ то́лько моне́ты.
> *And I've only got coins.*

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**English also has words that are plural by birth**: "scissors", "trousers",
"glasses" itself. So **очки́** won't surprise you. What's genuinely new is
*which* words each language puts in that group, and they don't match:

| Word | English | Russian |
|---|---|---|
| watch | singular: "the watch" | plural: **часы́** |
| glasses | plural: "the glasses" | plural: **очки́** |
| money | singular: "the money" | plural: **де́ньги** |

**Часы́** and **де́ньги** are the two that will trip you up: singular in
English, plural in Russian. There's no rule — you learn it the way you
learn any word's gender.

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud, with the stress at the end.*

> часы́ · очки́ · де́ньги · зе́ркало · моне́та · фотоаппара́т

### Б. Он, она́, оно́ и́ли они́?

*Which pronoun would you use for each word?*

1. зе́ркало
2. часы́
3. моне́та
4. де́ньги
5. фотоаппара́т

### В. У меня́ есть…

*Fill in with они́ or the matching pronoun.*

1. У меня́ есть часы́. Вот ______.
2. У тебя́ есть зе́ркало? Да, вот ______.
3. У меня́ есть де́ньги. Вот ______.

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Six objects, six sentences.** Say out loud whether you have each of
today's objects, with **У меня́ есть** or, if you don't, just **Нет** —the
full way to say "no" is still ahead:

> У меня́ есть часы́. Вот они́. · Очки́? Нет.

Then **record yourself** saying the three plural-only words and listen for
where the stress falls: at the end on all three, except **де́ньги**.

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> У меня́ есть часы́. · Вот они́. · У тебя́ есть де́ньги?
>
> **I know:** часы́, очки́ and де́ньги have no singular in Russian and always take они́, even when the English is singular.
