> **Today** you learn to build any number from 20 to 49, even though you only memorise four words. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - say **два́дцать**, **три́дцать** and **со́рок**;
> - build 21 to 49 by saying the tens and the units one after another;
> - say your age or a two-digit phone number.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

From 1 to 10, from memory and quick:

> оди́н, два, три, четы́ре, пять, шесть, семь, во́семь, де́вять, де́сять.

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

**Со́рок** is today's oddity: it doesn't follow the pattern of the other
tens.

| Word | Say it |
|---|---|
| два́дцать | **DVAT**-tsat' |
| три́дцать | **TRIT**-tsat' |
| со́рок | **SO**-rak |

In **два́дцать** and **три́дцать** the **д** goes voiceless before **ц**:
you don't hear a separate д.

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat:*

> два́дцать — три́дцать — со́рок
> два́дцать семь — три́дцать оди́н — со́рок два

## 3. New (8–18 min)

**Three new tens, and you already know 1 to 9 from unit 4.** With those
seven words you can build any number from 20 to 49:

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| два́дцать | **DVAT**-tsat' | twenty |
| три́дцать | **TRIT**-tsat' | thirty |
| со́рок | **SO**-rak | forty |

**You say the tens and then the units, with nothing in between**, written
as two separate words every time:

> 21 → два́дцать оди́н
> 27 → два́дцать семь
> 34 → три́дцать четы́ре
> 42 → со́рок два

No "and" between the two words — unlike English, which can go either way
("twenty-seven" or informally "twenty and seven" doesn't really happen, so
this one's not too bad for you.

> — Твой но́мер телефо́на?
> *Your phone number?*
> — Со́рок два, два́дцать семь…
> *Forty-two, twenty-seven…*

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**English uses a hyphen, Russian uses a plain space between two whole
words.** "Twenty-seven" reads as tightly joined; **два́дцать семь** are
always two separate words, whatever the tens:

| English | Russian |
|---|---|
| twenty-seven (hyphenated) | два́дцать семь (two words) |
| thirty-one | три́дцать оди́н |

There's no connector to add and no hyphen to write: just say the two words
one after the other.

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud.*

> два́дцать · три́дцать · со́рок · два́дцать семь · три́дцать оди́н · со́рок два

### Б. Как по-ру́сски?

*Say the number in Russian.*

1. 20
2. 30
3. 40
4. 25
5. 38
6. 49

### В. Переведи́те.

*Translate into English.*

1. Со́рок четы́ре.
2. Два́дцать де́вять.
3. Три́дцать шесть.

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Your phone number.** Say it aloud digit by digit, using today's tens
wherever they show up. If yours has none from 20 to 49, make one up that
does and say it three times.

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> два́дцать · три́дцать · со́рок · два́дцать семь · со́рок два
>
> **I know:** you say the tens and then the units, one after the other, with nothing in between.

Tomorrow: fifty to one hundred.
