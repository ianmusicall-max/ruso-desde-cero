> **Today** you say what you do for a living — a question that comes up far earlier in Russia than it does in Britain or North America. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - ask **Кем вы рабо́таете?** and **Кем ты рабо́таешь?**;
> - answer with a single word: **Я врач**;
> - name five jobs and say that you don't work.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

Say your unit 3 introduction aloud, all of it:

> — Здра́вствуйте! Меня́ зову́т… Я из… Я говорю́ по-англи́йски и немно́го по-ру́сски.

Today you add one more line, and it's the one people ask most.

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

The key word of the unit has two **о** and only one of them sounds [o]:

| Word | ✗ As it's written | ✓ As it's said |
|---|---|---|
| рабо́та | ~~ra-bo-ta~~ | ra-**BO**-ta |
| рабо́тать | ~~ra-bo-tat~~ | ra-**BO**-tat' |

The first **о** is unstressed, so it sounds [a]: the unit 1 rule, the one you
never stop applying. And the **-ть** at the end is a soft т — tongue against
the roof of the mouth, as in **пять**.

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat:*

> рабо́та — рабо́тать — рабо́таю — рабо́таешь
> врач — по́вар — учи́тель

## 3. New (8–18 min)

**The question.** Two forms, the **вы** one and the **ты** one:

> — Кем вы рабо́таете?
> *What do you do for a living?* (polite)
> — Я врач.
> *I'm a doctor.*

> — Кем ты рабо́таешь?
> *What do you do?* (to a friend)
> — Я по́вар.
> *I'm a cook.*

**Кем** is the form **кто** takes in this question. Today it comes in as a
block, like **Меня́ зову́т**: why it changes is A2 material. What *is* this
unit's business is **рабо́тать**, the verb, which you'll learn in full
tomorrow.

**And the answer has no verb.** None at all:

| | |
|---|---|
| Я врач. | I'm a doctor. |
| Он учи́тель. | He's a teacher. |
| Ки́ра — врач. | Kira is a doctor. |

With a name in front you write the dash, as in **Анто́н — студе́нт** back in
unit 1.

**Today's five jobs:**

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| врач | vrach | doctor |
| по́вар | **PO**-var | cook, chef |
| учи́тель | u-**CHEE**-tyel' | teacher (of children) |
| преподава́тель | pri-pa-da-**VA**-tyel' | teacher, lecturer (of adults) |
| инжене́р | in-zhi-**NYER** | engineer |

**Учи́тель** teaches children; **преподава́тель** teaches adults. Tom teaches
English to grown-ups at a language school, so he's a
**преподава́тель**.

**And to say you don't:**

> — Кем ты рабо́таешь?
> — Я не рабо́таю. Я студе́нт.
> *I don't work. I'm a student.*

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**English puts in two words that Russian doesn't have.** One is the verb
*to be*, which has no present tense in Russian — you saw that with **Э́то
кот** — and the other is the article:

| You mean | ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|---|
| I'm a doctor. | ~~Я есть врач.~~ | Я врач. |
| I'm an engineer. | ~~Я оди́н инжене́р.~~ | Я инжене́р. |

**And "I work as a cook" doesn't go across word for word.** English has both
*I'm a cook* and *I work as a cook*; at this level Russian has only the
first:

| You mean | ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|---|
| I work as a cook. | ~~Я рабо́таю как по́вар.~~ | Я по́вар. |

**And the question doesn't start with "what".** English asks *what do you
do?*; Russian asks with **кем**, not with **что**:
~~Что ты рабо́таешь?~~ → **Кем ты рабо́таешь?**

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud. Mark the stressed syllable of each word with your finger.*

> рабо́та · рабо́тать · врач · по́вар · учи́тель · преподава́тель · инжене́р

### Б. Как по-ру́сски?

*Say it in Russian. No verb and no article.*

1. I'm an engineer.
2. She's a doctor.
3. Anton is a teacher.
4. I don't work.
5. What do you do? (to a friend)

### В. Найди́те оши́бку.

*One sentence in each pair is wrong. Which one?*

1. а) Я есть по́вар. · б) Я по́вар.
2. а) Кем ты рабо́таешь? · б) Что ты рабо́таешь?
3. а) Он оди́н врач. · б) Он врач.
4. а) Я не рабо́таю. · б) Я нет рабо́таю.

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Answer it yourself.** Say this aloud, three times, without thinking:

> — Кем ты рабо́таешь?
> — Я…

If your job isn't in today's list, never mind: say it in English inside the
Russian frame (**Я…**) and note it down to look up. Six more arrive in
lesson 3.

Then **record yourself** and check two things: that **рабо́таешь** comes out
[ra-**BO**-tash], with the first о turned into [a], and that no *есть* has
crept in.

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> Кем вы рабо́таете? · Кем ты рабо́таешь? · Я врач. · Я не рабо́таю.
>
> **I know:** a job is said with no verb and no article, and the question uses **кем**, not **что**.

Tomorrow: **рабо́тать** in full, all six forms.
