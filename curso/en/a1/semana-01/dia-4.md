> **Today** you learn the letter И and your first Russian conversation: introducing yourself. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - read **И** and the names **А́нна**, **Анто́н**, **Макси́м** and **Мари́на**;
> - ask **Как тебя́ зову́т?** and answer **Меня́ зову́т…**;
> - say **О́чень прия́тно** when you meet someone.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

Read aloud. In each word, find the stressed о first: all the others sound
[a].

> Вот метро́. · Там рестора́н? — Нет. · Вот Москва́. · Там окно́.

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

One new letter, and an easy one to hear:

| Letter | Looks like | Sounds | As in |
|---|---|---|---|
| И и | a backwards N | [i] | m**ee**t (short, no glide) |

Like **е**, **и** softens the consonant in front of it a little: **ни** is
close to the *ni* in *onion*, with the tongue pressed to the roof of the
mouth.

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat:*

> и — ни — ми — ти — ки — ри
> ин — им — ит — ик — ис — ив

And a surprise: **и** on its own is a word. It means "and":
**Анто́н и А́нна** — Anton and Anna.

## 3. New (8–18 min)

Your first conversation. Two people meet at a party:

> — Приве́т! Как тебя́ зову́т?
> *Hi! What's your name?*
> — Меня́ зову́т Анто́н. А тебя́?
> *My name's Anton. And yours?*
> — А́нна.
> *Anna.*
> — О́чень прия́тно!
> *Nice to meet you!*

You learn this dialogue **by ear, as a block**: it has letters you don't know
yet (б, з, я, ч, ь), and that's fine. In ten days you'll read all of it.
Today, listen and repeat until it comes out without thinking:

| Phrase | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Как тебя́ зову́т? | kak ti-**BYA** za-**VOOT** | What's your name? |
| Меня́ зову́т… | mi-**NYA** za-**VOOT** | My name is… |
| А тебя́? | a ti-**BYA** | And yours? |
| О́чень прия́тно. | **O**-chin pri-**YAT**-na | Nice to meet you. |

This is the **informal** form, for friends, young people and children. The
polite one comes in unit 3.

And three words you can already read with **и**:

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| такси́ | tak-**SEE** | taxi |
| кино́ | ki-**NO** | cinema, the movies |
| тури́ст | too-**REEST** | tourist |

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**Russian doesn't say "my name is".** The everyday phrase is "me (they)
call…", and the verb **зову́т** ("they call") never changes, whoever you're
talking about:

| You mean | ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|---|
| My name's Anna. | ~~Я зову́т А́нна.~~ | Меня́ зову́т А́нна. |
| What's your name? | ~~Как ты зову́т?~~ | Как тебя́ зову́т? |

It's the shape of English *people call me Annie*: the person isn't the one
doing the calling, but the one being called. (**Моё и́мя…**, "my name is",
does exist, but it sounds like filling in a form.)

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud: Russian names and three words with и.*

> Ни́на — Мари́на — Макси́м — Анто́н — А́нна — Ве́ра — такси́ — кино́ — тури́ст

### Б. Расста́вьте по поря́дку.

*Put the dialogue in order.*

> а) — О́чень прия́тно!
> б) — Приве́т! Как тебя́ зову́т?
> в) — Мари́на.
> г) — Меня́ зову́т Макси́м. А тебя́?

### В. Переведи́те.

*Translate into Russian.*

1. My name's Nina.
2. And yours?
3. Nice to meet you.
4. Anton and Anna.

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Introduce yourself three times.** Imagine three meetings: a party, the
metro, a class. Say the whole dialogue, both voices, with your real name. If
your name has letters you don't know yet, that's fine: you say it.

Then **record yourself** on your phone and listen back. Check two things:
that **зову́т** sounds [za-VOOT], with the unstressed о turned into [a], and
that **тебя́** is one quick [ti-BYA], not "teh-bee-ah".

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> Как тебя́ зову́т? · Меня́ зову́т… · А тебя́? · О́чень прия́тно!
>
> **I know:** А, О, К, М, Т, С, Р, У, Х, В, Е, Н, И — 13 of 33 letters.

Tomorrow: **Э** and **Д**, and the question you ask with your voice alone.
