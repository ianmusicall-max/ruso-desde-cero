A1 · Unit 4 · Week 8 · Day 5 · Lesson

My city

Мой го́род

Draft: this lesson hasn't had its final Russian review yet. It may change.

Today you'll describe a map using what you already know. Start with a new phrase, Э́то мой го́род: “This is my city.”

1Warm-up0–3 min

Draw three boxes and put a bank on the left, a pharmacy in the centre and a café on the right. Without looking ahead, ask and answer:

Где банк? · Где апте́ка? · Где кафе́?

Answer with just the location. Then do it again, looking at the drawing instead of the words.

2Pronunciation3–8 min

Yesterday you learned how one consonant can change next to another. Warm up with two examples before you use them to describe a map:

где → [где́] · вокза́л → [вагза́л]

In вокза́л, the written к sounds like [g] before з. Say the word on its own, then the whole question. In Где вокза́л?, stress где and let your pitch fall at the end.

Где вокза́л? — Вот он, спра́ва.

3New8–18 min

Imagine Tom is showing you a drawing of his city. See what he says about each part:

Э́то мой го́род.
В це́нтре — метро́.
Сле́ва — банк.
Спра́ва — вокза́л.
Там кафе́.

“This is my city. The metro is in the centre. There's a bank on the left and a railway station on the right. There's a café over there.”

Learn мой го́род as one chunk, “my city”. Leave the other forms of “my” for later. After the opening line, use location + name to describe what you see. Learn в це́нтре as a chunk too, “in the centre”.

On the drawing You can say
bank on the left Сле́ва — банк.
pharmacy on the right Спра́ва — апте́ка.
café in the centre В це́нтре — кафе́.

If someone points at the pictures of cafés and asks Ско́лько?, “How many?”, you can answer Два. For now, say the number on its own.

⚠️ Contrast with English

English normally gives you both “there's” and “a” in “There's a bank on the left”. On a Russian map, Сле́ва — банк already does the job. Russian doesn't have an article to put before банк. Make a short pause at the dash, then say the name of the place.

4Practice18–25 min

А. Где э́то?

Where is it? Match each phrase with Tom's map.

  1. Сле́ва. → а) метро́ · б) банк · в) вокза́л
  2. В це́нтре. → а) метро́ · б) банк · в) вокза́л
  3. Спра́ва. → а) метро́ · б) банк · в) вокза́л

Б. Скажи́те по-ру́сски

Say it in Russian. Use the opening phrase or the location + name pattern.

  1. This is my city.
  2. There's a pharmacy on the right.
  3. There's a café in the centre.
  4. There's a bank on the left.

В. Ско́лько?

Answer with the number in Russian. The drawing tells you what you're counting.

  1. You see two cafés. — Ско́лько?
  2. You see three banks. — Ско́лько?
  3. You see one railway station. — Ско́лько?

5Your turn25–29 min

Add another café outside the three boxes in your warm-up map. Introduce the map with Э́то мой го́род, then describe the centre, left and right in three sentences. Point to the cafés, ask Ско́лько?, and answer. Record yourself and check whether a listener could redraw the three boxes from what you said.

6Wrap-up29–30 min

I can now say: Э́то мой го́род. В це́нтре — метро́. Сле́ва — банк.

I can now: introduce a city and point out three places on a map.
