> **Today** you learn to say what you have, with no verb "to have" at all. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - say **У меня́ есть** and the name of the object;
> - ask **У тебя́ есть…?** and answer yes or no;
> - name six objects from a room.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

Six objects you already know, with their gender:

> ла́мпа — она́ · ключ — он · су́мка — она́ · телефо́н — он · окно́ — оно́ · кни́га — она́

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

Two of today's words have two consonants stuck together:

| Word | Say it |
|---|---|
| крова́ть | kra-**VAT'** |
| фотогра́фия | fa-ta-**GRA**-fi-ya |

In **крова́ть** the к and р sit right next to each other, with no vowel
between: don't say "ka-ra-vat'". And in **фотогра́фия** three unstressed
syllables come before the stress: all of them weak and quick.

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat:*

> стол — стул — крова́ть
> шкаф — письмо́ — фотогра́фия

## 3. New (8–18 min)

**There is no verb "to have" in Russian.** To say something is yours, you
say it exists "by you":

> **У меня́ есть** стол.
> *I've got a table.* (literally: "by me there is table")

**У меня́ есть** is a block, like **Меня́ зову́т**: you learn it whole.
"Меня́" doesn't change here either, though tomorrow you'll see why it has
that form.

Six new objects, with their gender:

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| стол | stol | table |
| стул | stool | chair |
| крова́ть | kra-**VAT'** | bed |
| шкаф | shkaf | wardrobe |
| письмо́ | pis'-**MO** | letter |
| фотогра́фия | fa-ta-**GRA**-fi-ya | photo |

> — У меня́ есть стол и стул.
> *I've got a table and a chair.*
> — А у меня́ есть то́лько крова́ть.
> *And I've only got a bed.*

**The question and the short answer.** To ask if someone has something:

> — У тебя́ есть шкаф?
> *Have you got a wardrobe?*
> — Да, есть.
> *Yes, I have.* (literally: "yes, there is")
> — Нет.
> *No.*

Today you only learn the short answer, **Да, есть** / **Нет**. The full way
to deny it — with the object's ending changed — comes later; for now, a
bare **Нет** is enough.

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**You're looking for a verb that doesn't exist here.** English says "I
have" and expects a word that conjugates like "eat" or "read". In Russian,
**есть** doesn't conjugate by person at all — it's the same word for "I
have", "you have" and "he has" — and what changes is the pronoun in front
of it:

| You mean | ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|---|
| I've got a table. | ~~Я име́ю стол.~~ | У меня́ есть стол. |
| Have you got a wardrobe? | ~~Ты име́ешь шкаф?~~ | У тебя́ есть шкаф? |

**Име́ть** does exist in Russian, but only in abstract phrases (**име́ть
значе́ние**, "to matter"): for objects, it's always **у меня́ есть**.

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud, with the stress clearly marked.*

> стол · стул · крова́ть · шкаф · письмо́ · фотогра́фия

### Б. У меня́ есть…

*Say what you have, with each of today's objects.*

> стол → У меня́ есть стол.

1. стул
2. крова́ть
3. шкаф
4. письмо́
5. фотогра́фия

### В. Да и́ли нет?

*Answer with Да, есть or Нет, as the question asks.*

1. У тебя́ есть стол? (yes)
2. У тебя́ есть шкаф? (no)
3. У тебя́ есть крова́ть? (yes)

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Your room in six sentences.** Look around and say which of today's items
you really have, with **У меня́ есть**:

> У меня́ есть стол. У меня́ есть стул.

And for the ones you don't have, just **Нет**, as in exercise В. Then ask
an imaginary person about the six objects, one by one, and answer yourself
in that person's voice.

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> У меня́ есть стол. · У тебя́ есть шкаф? · Да, есть. · Нет.
>
> **I know:** Russian has no verb "to have": you say something exists by you, with **у меня́ есть**.
