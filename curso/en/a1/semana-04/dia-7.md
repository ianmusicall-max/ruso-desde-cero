> **Today** the story of Camila and Tom goes on. You read this episode entirely in Russian, with what you know from units 1 and 2: asking what something is, saying it's not this but that, and answering that you don't know. By the end you'll have found two more mistakes, and one of them is yours again.

## 1. Before you read (0–5 min)

A month has gone by since episode 1. Camila and Tom now say hello on the
stairs, and Kira has had them over for tea a couple of times. Today
**Ми́ша** (Misha), Kira's son, steps in: he's 9, and he corrects without
mercy, the way children do.

**The neighbour's tea.** In Russia, when someone knocks at your door, it's
customary to offer them tea: **Чай?** is almost a way of saying "come in".

Read the title: **Э́то не ша́пка, а кот!** Before you read, note down in one
line what you think happens.

## 2. Text (5–18 min)

### Эпизо́д 2. Э́то не ша́пка, а кот!

*An October morning. Tom is going down the stairs and, on one step, he finds something grey, big and furry.*

> **Том:** Ой! Что э́то? Ша́пка?

*Tom thinks it's Misha's. He goes up and knocks at Kira's door.*

> **Ки́ра:** Том! Приве́т! Как дела́?
> **Том:** Хорошо́, спаси́бо. Ки́ра, вот, пожа́луйста: ша́пка. Ми́ша?
> **Ки́ра:** Ша́пка? Мо́жет быть…

*Kira takes the hat. The hat moves.*

> **«Ша́пка»:** Мя́у!
> **Том:** Ой!
> **Ки́ра:** Ба́рсик! Том, э́то не ша́пка, а кот!

*Behind his mother, Misha appears, in his pyjamas.*

> **Ми́ша:** Ма́ма, кто там?
> **Том:** Здра́вствуйте, Ми́ша! Как вы?
> **Ми́ша:** «Вы»? Ха-ха́! Я не «вы», а Ми́ша! Приве́т, Том!
> **Ки́ра:** Ми́ша, Ба́рсик — ша́пка!
> **Ми́ша:** Ша́пка? Ха-ха-ха́!

*Camila comes down the stairs. Misha now has Bársik in his arms, curled up in a ball: he looks like a hat again.*

> **Ками́ла:** Приве́т! Что случи́лось?
> **Ми́ша:** Ками́ла, а э́то? Ша́пка и́ли кот?
> **Ками́ла:** Нет зна́ю…
> **Ми́ша:** Не «нет зна́ю», а «не зна́ю»! И э́то не ша́пка, а кот!
> **Ки́ра:** Ми́ша! Чай? Ко́фе?
> **Ками́ла:** Мне чай, пожа́луйста.
> **Том:** Мне ко́фе, пожа́луйста.
> **Ки́ра:** Вот, пожа́луйста.

*Bársik jumps onto the sofa and curls up into a ball. Tom looks at him.*

> **Том:** Ша́пка?
> **Ки́ра, Ками́ла и Ми́ша:** Нет! Кот!

Read it twice: the first time straight through; the second, aloud, with the
intonation of each question.

## 3. Words (18–23 min)

The whole episode comes from units 1 and 2 except one word, which explains
itself:

| Russian | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ха-ха́ | ha ha | Ша́пка? Ха-ха-ха́! |

And these four phrases from unit 2 are the ones that make the scene. If one
doesn't come to you, go back to its day:

| Russian | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Мо́жет быть… | Maybe… | Ша́пка? Мо́жет быть… |
| Э́то не…, а… | It's not…, it's… | Э́то не ша́пка, а кот! |
| Не зна́ю. | I don't know. | Ша́пка и́ли кот? — Не зна́ю. |
| Что случи́лось? | What's happened? | Приве́т! Что случи́лось? |

## 4. Task (23–29 min)

### А. Ве́рно и́ли неве́рно?

*True or false?*

1. Tom finds the hat on the stairs.
2. The hat is Misha's.
3. Tom calls Misha **вы**.
4. Camila doesn't know whether it's a hat or a cat.
5. Tom asks for tea.

### Б. Найди́те две оши́бки.

*This time Misha corrects the mistakes. One is typical of a Spanish speaker and the other of an English speaker. Which one is yours? How does Misha put it?*

### В. Чита́йте по роля́м.

*Read Misha's scene aloud, playing every part yourself. Mind three intonations: **Ша́пка?** jumps on "ша" (ИК-3); **Кто там?** leans on **кто** and falls (ИК-2); **А э́то?** rises and stays up (ИК-4).*

## 5. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **Now you know:** Bársik passes himself off as a hat, Misha forgives no mistake, and you speak to a child with **ты**.

Tomorrow unit 3 begins: where you're from, which languages you speak, and
Camila's first Russian form.

## Answers

**А.** 1 true · 2 false: it isn't a hat, it's Bársik · 3 true · 4 true · 5
false: he asks for coffee

**Б.** Tom's is yours: English has just one "you", and Tom calls a 9-year-old
**вы**. Children, family and friends get **ты**: **Приве́т, Ми́ша! Как
дела́?** Camila's is the Spanish speaker's: **Нет зна́ю**. In Spanish "no"
is always "no"; in Russian "I don't know" takes **не**, just as Misha says:
**Не зна́ю**. (English helps you here: **нет** is "no" and **не** is "not".)
