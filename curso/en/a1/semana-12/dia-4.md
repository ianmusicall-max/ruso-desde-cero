> **Today** you learn to ask and say someone's age, with two new words that aren't verbs. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - ask "how old are you?" two ways;
> - say your age with **мне … лет**;
> - understand **тебе́** and **вам** without treating them as separate grammar.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

Two numbers from yesterday, out loud:

> се́мьдесят · девяно́сто

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

**Лет** rhymes with "нет": the **е** sounds like [ye] and the final **т**
doesn't soften.

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat:*

> ско́лько — тебе́ — лет
> ско́лько тебе́ лет?

## 3. New (8–18 min)

You already know **мне** from "Мне нра́вится," and "Мне … лет" isn't
entirely new either: what's added today is the question itself and its
two forms, **тебе́** and **вам**.

**You've already seen вам, without knowing what it was**: back in the
unit-2 café you heard "Что вам?" and "А вам?" as sealed chunks, never
taken apart. Today you finally find out what it is: the same form as
**тебе́**, just formal.

| Word | It sounds like | It means |
|---|---|---|
| ско́лько | **SKOL'**-ka | how many |
| лет | lyet | years (with a number) |
| тебе́ | ti-**BIE** | to you (informal) |
| вам | vam | to you (formal / plural) |

**The question is a fixed chunk**: there's no need to analyze why
**тебе́** and **вам** have that form —that's the dative case, which
comes much later. For now you learn them whole, just like you already
learned **мне**:

> — Ско́лько тебе́ лет?
> *How old are you?*
> — Мне три́дцать лет.
> *I'm thirty.*
>
> — Ско́лько вам лет?
> *How old are you?* (formal)
> — Мне пятьдеся́т шесть лет.
> *I'm fifty-six.*

**Тебе́** goes with **ты**, and **вам** with **вы**: the same
trust-based distinction you already know from unit 3.

**Лет** isn't the only form: with "1" you say **год**, and with 2, 3 or
4, **го́да**. For now it's enough to recognize them if you hear them;
the full rule for when to use each one comes with the plural of the
noun, in unit 12.

| Word | It sounds like | It means |
|---|---|---|
| год | got | year (with "1") |
| го́да | **GO**-da | years (with 2, 3, 4) |

> Мне оди́н год. *I'm one year old.*
> Мне три го́да. *I'm three years old.*

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**English uses the verb "to be"; Russian uses no verb at all.** "I'm
thirty" is said with **мне** (to me) + the number + **лет**, with no
equivalent of "am":

| English | Russian |
|---|---|
| I am 30 (years old). | **Мне** три́дцать лет. |
| How old are you? | **Ско́лько тебе́ лет?** |

If you go looking for a verb in the Russian sentence, you won't find
one: your age isn't something you "are" in Russian — it's simply a
number of years that "is to you."

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud.*

> Ско́лько тебе́ лет? · Ско́лько вам лет? · Мне три́дцать лет.

### Б. Тебе́ и́ли вам?

*Which one goes with whom?*

1. You ask a child → а) тебе́ · б) вам
2. You ask your boss → а) тебе́ · б) вам
3. You ask your sister → а) тебе́ · б) вам
4. You ask a woman you don't know → а) тебе́ · б) вам

### В. Напиши́те отве́т.

*Write your own age as a full sentence.*

1. Мне ___ лет.

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Ask someone.** If you have someone to speak Russian with (or can
picture the scene), ask "Ско́лько тебе́ лет?" and answer with your real
age.

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **Now I can say:**
> Ско́лько тебе́ лет? · Ско́лько вам лет? · Мне ___ лет.
>
> **Now I know:** how to ask someone's age with **ско́лько**, and that Russian uses no verb to say it.
