A1 · Unit 2 · Week 4 · Day 2 · Lesson

Here you are: offering and giving

Вот, пожа́луйста

Draft: this lesson hasn't had its final Russian review yet. It may change.

Today you order in a café: Мне чай, пожа́луйста. And you hand over what's ordered: Вот, пожа́луйста. By the end you'll be able to:

  • order a drink without any verb;
  • offer and give politely: Что вам? · А вам?;
  • name five more drinks, one of them very Russian.

1Warm-up0–3 min

Answer aloud with three different points on yesterday's scale, and react to each:

— Как дела́? — Отли́чно! — Здо́рово!
— Как дела́? — Так себе́. — Что случи́лось?
— Как дела́? — Норма́льно. А у тебя́?

2Pronunciation3–8 min

Today's drinks, with the unit's rules. Find the stressed syllable first:

Word Said Why
молоко́ [ma-la-KO] three о's and only the last one is stressed
лимона́д [li-ma-NAT] the unstressed о; the final д loses its voice
вино́ [vi-NO] stress at the end
квас [kvas] one syllable, no trap
кака́о [ka-KA-o] the exception

Кака́о is an exception: in a few foreign words, like this one and ра́дио, the unstressed final о hardly weakens.

Слу́шайте и повторя́йте. Listen and repeat. First the offer, which rises; then the answer, which falls:

Чай? — Да, пожа́луйста.
Молоко́? — Нет, спаси́бо.
Кака́о? — Да, пожа́луйста!

3New8–18 min

In a café:

— Здра́вствуйте! Что вам?
Hello. What can I get you?
— Мне чай, пожа́луйста.
Tea for me, please.
— Вот, пожа́луйста. А вам?
Here you are. And for you?
— Мне кака́о.
Hot chocolate for me.
— Вот, пожа́луйста.
Here you are.
— Спаси́бо!
Thank you!

Four blocks, to learn whole, without taking them apart:

  • Что вам? — what the person serving asks: "what can I get you?".
  • Мне… — "for me…": that's how you order, with no verb.
  • А вам? — "and for you?", to the next person.
  • Вот, пожа́луйста. — "here you are", as you hand something over. You already knew пожа́луйста as "please" and "you're welcome": this is its third job.

And the words:

Word Say it Means
Что вам? shto VAM What can I get you?
мне mnye for me, to me
А вам? a VAM And for you?
молоко́ ma-la-KO milk
вино́ vi-NO wine
лимона́д li-ma-NAT fizzy soft drink
квас kvas kvass
кака́о ka-KA-o cocoa, hot chocolate

Квас is the most Russian thing on today's menu: it's made by fermenting rye bread, and it's refreshing and almost alcohol-free. In summer it's drunk ice-cold.

A trap, so it doesn't catch you. Today you order drinks that don't change. Words ending in -а do change when you order them: it isn't Мне вода́ but Мне во́ду. That comes in unit 8. Until then, point and say: Мне э́то, пожа́луйста.

⚠️ Contrast with English

English orders with a verb; Russian doesn't. "I'll have a tea", "Can I get a coffee?": Russian says only "to me, tea": Мне чай. What doesn't work is starting with "I":

You mean ✗ Don't say ✓ Say
I'll have a tea. Я чай. Мне чай.
I'll have a coffee, please. Я ко́фе, пожа́луйста. Мне ко́фе, пожа́луйста.

And лимона́д is British lemonade, not American. It's any sweet fizzy soft drink, lemon or not. If you want lemon juice with water and sugar, лимона́д isn't what you'll get.

4Practice18–25 min

А. Чита́йте.

Read aloud, with the rules.

молоко́ — лимона́д — вино́ — кака́о — квас — чай — ко́фе — сок

Б. Закажи́те.

Order in Russian, with мне and пожа́луйста.

  1. a tea
  2. a juice
  3. milk
  4. a kvass
  5. a hot chocolate

В. Расста́вьте по поря́дку.

Put the dialogue in order.

а) Вот, пожа́луйста. б) Спаси́бо! в) Здра́вствуйте! Что вам? г) Мне ко́фе, пожа́луйста.

5Your turn25–29 min

Your Russian café. You're the waiter serving two friends, Ни́на and Анто́н. Play all three parts aloud and record yourself:

— Здра́вствуйте! Что вам? — Мне … — Вот, пожа́луйста. А вам? — …

Listen: does молоко́ sound [ma-la-KO], with the first two о's turned into [a]? Then order something ending in -а by pointing at it: Мне э́то, пожа́луйста.

6Wrap-up29–30 min

I can now say:
Что вам? · Мне чай, пожа́луйста. · Вот, пожа́луйста. · А вам? · молоко́, вино́, лимона́д, квас, кака́о

I know: you order with no verb, "to me, tea"; and пожа́луйста also works for handing things over.
