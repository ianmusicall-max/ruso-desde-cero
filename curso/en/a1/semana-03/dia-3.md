> **Today** you learn the difference that changes the sound of Russian most: nearly every consonant has a hard and a soft version. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - tell, from the letter after it, whether a consonant is hard or soft;
> - hear the difference between **лук** and **люк**, **брат** and **брать**;
> - name seven words for home: **мать**, **дверь**, **соль**, **мя́со**…

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

Read aloud, fading the unstressed е's and я's:

> метро́ — телефо́н — тетра́дь — язы́к — рестора́н

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

**A soft consonant is said with the middle of the tongue raised to the roof of
the mouth**, as if a *y* were about to follow. A hard one, with the tongue low
and back. What tells you which is **the letter after it**:

| After it comes… | The consonant is… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| а, о, у, э, ы, or nothing | hard | **ма**, **лу**, **рад** |
| я, ё, ю, е, и, or ь | soft | **мя**, **лю**, **ряд**, **мать** |

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat: first the hard one, then the soft one.*

> ма — мя · ну — ню · ло — лё · ты — ти · да — дя
> лук — люк · рад — ряд · брат — брать

Three fixed rules that save you a lot of doubt:

- **ж, ш, ц are always hard**, even before и: **маши́на** is [ma-SHY-na],
  not "ma-SHEE-na", and **цирк** is [tsyrk].
- **ч and щ are always soft**, even before а: **чай**, **борщ**.
- After **ж, ш, ч, щ** the **ь** changes nothing: **ночь** sounds the same as
  it would without it. It's pure spelling.

## 3. New (8–18 min)

Seven words for home. In the "Say it" column, the apostrophe **'** marks a
soft final consonant:

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| мать | mat' | mother |
| дверь | dvyer' | door |
| соль | sol' | salt |
| ночь | noch | night |
| ло́шадь | **LO**-shat' | horse |
| мя́со | **MYA**-sa | meat |
| лук | look | onion |

> — Что э́то?
> *What's this?*
> — Э́то мя́со.
> *Meat.*
> — А э́то? Соль?
> *And this? Salt?*
> — Нет, э́то не соль, а сок.
> *No, it's not salt, it's juice.*

A pair for your ear: **лук** (onion) and **люк** (a hatch, a manhole cover).
Only one thing changes: whether the л is hard or soft.

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**English has no soft consonants, but you can borrow two.** The *ny* in the
middle of *canyon* is a soft **н**: say *canyon*, stop on the *ny*, and you
have the end of **день**. The *lli* in *million* is close to a soft **л**.

**The trap is the end of a word.** English never ends a word on a soft
consonant, so it's tempting to say **мать** like *mat*. And the ь changes
words:

| Without ь | With ь |
|---|---|
| **брат** — brother | **брать** — to take |
| **у́гол** — corner | **у́голь** — coal |

Finish the soft one as if a *y* were about to follow and never arrives:
"mat'".

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read each pair aloud: hard, then soft.*

> ма — мя · лу — лю · ро — рё · ны — ни
> лук — люк · рад — ряд · брат — брать · у́гол — у́голь

### Б. Твёрдый и́ли мя́гкий?

*Is the bold consonant hard or soft?*

1. **м**я́со
2. **л**ук
3. две**р**ь
4. **н**очь
5. со**л**ь
6. ма**ш**и́на

### В. Соедини́те.

*Match each word with its translation.*

1. мать
2. дверь
3. соль
4. ночь
5. ло́шадь

а) night · б) horse · в) mother · г) salt · д) door

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**The mirror test.** In front of a mirror, say **ма — мя**, **лу — лю**,
**брат — брать**. On the soft one your tongue rises towards the roof of your
mouth and your lips spread a little, as if smiling: you should be able to see
it.

Then record yourself reading today's seven words and listen to the endings:
can you hear the soft sound in **мать**, **дверь**, **соль**, **ло́шадь**?

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> мать, дверь, соль, ночь, ло́шадь, мя́со, лук · Э́то не соль, а сок.
>
> **I know:** я, ё, ю, е, и and ь soften; ж, ш, ц are always hard; ч, щ, always soft.
