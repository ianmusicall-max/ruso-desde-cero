> **Today** you learn when to use **ты** and when **вы**, and how to switch from one to the other: **Дава́йте на «ты»!** By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - choose **ты** or **вы** with anyone;
> - suggest switching to **ты**, or ask whether you may;
> - write **Вы** with a capital letter where it belongs.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

The whole verb, aloud, with the beat at the end:

> я говорю́ · ты говори́шь · он говори́т · мы говори́м · вы говори́те · они́ говоря́т

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

**Today's phrases, in one go.** **на «ты»** and **на «вы»** are said like a
single word, with the beat on the pronoun:

> на «ты» [na-TY] · на «вы» [na-VY]

**мо́жно**: the ж is hard and the final о weakens: [MOZH-na].

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat. The question jumps on **мо́ж**; the suggestion falls:*

> Мо́жно на «ты»?
> Дава́йте на «ты»!
> Дава́й!

## 3. New (8–18 min)

**Ты** or **вы** depends on the person, and the starting rule is simple:

| ты | вы |
|---|---|
| family | an adult stranger |
| friends | an older person |
| a child | your teacher, the doctor, the boss |
| young people among themselves | whoever serves you in a shop or an office |
| | **any group**: two friends together are **вы** too |

And anyone you call by first name and patronymic, **Ве́ра Ива́новна**, you
address as **вы**.

**You switch from вы to ты by talking about it.** It's nearly always the
older or more senior person who suggests it, and the other agrees:

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| на «ты» | na-**TY** | on ты terms |
| на «вы» | na-**VY** | on вы terms |
| мо́жно | **MOZH**-na | it's allowed; may I? |
| Мо́жно на «ты»? | **MOZH**-na na-**TY** | Can we say ты? |
| Дава́йте на «ты»! | da-**VAI**-tye na-**TY** | Let's say ты! |
| Дава́й! | da-**VAI** | Let's!, OK! |

> — Анто́н, мо́жно на «ты»?
> *Anton, can we say ты?*
> — Коне́чно! Дава́й!
> *Of course! Let's!*

And in writing: in a letter or an email to one person, **Вы** is written with
a capital letter, as a sign of respect: **Ве́ра Ива́новна, как Вы?**

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**English has one "you", so the choice is new.** By default, an adult
stranger gets **вы**, even a young one, and **ты** waits until someone
suggests it. With a stranger, **ты** sounds disrespectful; with a child,
**вы** sounds comic: that was Tom's mistake with Misha.

**And "you" for a group is always вы**, however close the friends:

| You mean | ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|---|
| Where are you from? (to two friends) | ~~Отку́да ты?~~ | Отку́да вы? |
| Do you speak English? (to the cashier) | ~~Ты говори́шь по-англи́йски?~~ | Вы говори́те по-англи́йски? |

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud, with the right intonation.*

> Мо́жно на «ты»? — Коне́чно! · Дава́йте на «ты»! — Дава́й! · Мы на «ты». · Мы на «вы».

### Б. Ты и́ли вы?

*How do you address them?*

1. Your sister.
2. The doctor.
3. A six-year-old child.
4. Two friends, at once.
5. A friend's grandmother.
6. A classmate your own age.
7. The supermarket cashier.
8. Your cat.

### В. Как сказа́ть?

*What do you say?*

1. A new colleague your age calls you **вы**. You suggest **ты**, as a
   question.
2. You're the boss and you suggest **ты** to your team.
3. Someone suggests it to you, and you happily agree.

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Your first day at work.** Write a six-line dialogue with a new colleague:
you start on **вы** (**Здра́вствуйте! Как вас зову́т? Отку́да вы?**), one of
you suggests **ты** and you carry on with **ты** (**Отку́да ты? Ты говори́шь
по-англи́йски?**). Read it aloud and record yourself.

Listen: do you switch from **вы** to **ты** at the right moment, and not
before?

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> Мо́жно на «ты»? · Дава́йте на «ты»! · Дава́й! · Мы на «ты». · Мы на «вы».
>
> **I know:** an adult stranger gets **вы**; any group gets **вы**; and **ты** is suggested, never just taken.
