> **Today** you read and hand over a business card — in Russia they're swapped the moment two people meet through work. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - read a whole visitka: name, surname, job, company;
> - say **Вот моя́ визи́тка** and ask for the other person's;
> - give your address and your number.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

Yesterday's six verbs, in the **я** form:

> звоню́ · курю́ · учу́ · варю́ · стро́ю · лечу́

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

Today's words are a walking revision of both reduction rules:

| Word | Say it | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| а́дрес | **A**-dris | unstressed **е** closes to [i] |
| но́мер | **NO**-mir | unstressed **е** again |
| компа́ния | kam-**PA**-ni-ya | unstressed **о** sounds [a] |
| дире́ктор | di-**RYEK**-tar | unstressed **и** and **о** |

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat, with the strong syllable well marked:*

> а́дрес — но́мер — визи́тка
> компа́ния — дире́ктор — профе́ссия

## 3. New (8–18 min)

**A Russian card has four lines, always in this order:**

> **Петро́в Анто́н**
> инжене́р
> компа́ния «Метро́»
> тел. 8-495-…

Surname first, given name second: that's how it goes on documents and in
lists. Spoken, it's the other way round — **Анто́н Петро́в** — as you've known
since unit 3.

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| визи́тка | vi-**ZEET**-ka | business card |
| а́дрес | **A**-dris | address |
| но́мер | **NO**-mir | number |
| компа́ния | kam-**PA**-ni-ya | company |
| дире́ктор | di-**RYEK**-tar | director, manager |

**And the line you hand it over with:**

> — Вот моя́ визи́тка.
> *Here's my card.*
> — Спаси́бо. Кем вы рабо́таете?
> *Thank you. What do you do?*
> — Я дире́ктор.
> *I'm a director.*

**моя́** comes in today as a block, like **мой го́род** in unit 4: the full
set of possessives arrives in unit 7. For now, learn **моя́ визи́тка** as one
piece.

To give the details on their own, with no verb, as on a form:

> Фами́лия: Петро́в. И́мя: Анто́н. Профе́ссия: инжене́р.
> А́дрес: Москва́, у́лица Пу́шкина, 5.
> Но́мер: 8-495-…

Email addresses have their own way of being read aloud, and they arrive in
unit 7 together with the full phone number.

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**A card is not a ка́рта.** That's today's false friend, and you've had it in
front of you since unit 1:

| You mean | ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|---|
| business card | ~~ка́рта~~ | визи́тка |
| map | ~~визи́тка~~ | ка́рта |

**And фами́лия still means "surname".** You met it in unit 3 and it's back,
because on a card it's the first line: **фами́лия** never means "family".
Family is **семья́**, and that comes in unit 7.

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read the card aloud, line by line.*

> Ивано́ва Ки́ра
> врач
> компа́ния «Здоро́вье»
> тел. 8-495-…

### Б. Что э́то?

*Which line of the card is it?*

1. Петро́в → а) и́мя · б) фами́лия
2. перево́дчик → а) профе́ссия · б) компа́ния
3. Москва́, у́лица Пу́шкина, 5 → а) но́мер · б) а́дрес
4. 8-495 → а) но́мер · б) и́мя

### В. Допиши́те.

*Complete the dialogue.*

1. — Вот ______ визи́тка. — Спаси́бо!
2. — Кем вы ______? — Я дире́ктор.
3. — ______? — Москва́, у́лица Пу́шкина, 5. (the address line)

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Make your own card.** On a piece of paper, four lines in Russian: surname
and given name (in Cyrillic, as in the writing taster on the home page), job,
company or school, and the number.

Then introduce yourself aloud three times, as if you were at a conference:

> — Здра́вствуйте! Меня́ зову́т… Я… Вот моя́ визи́тка.

If you don't have a company, put your city. What matters is getting through
it without stopping.

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> Вот моя́ визи́тка. · Я дире́ктор. · А́дрес: Москва́… · Но́мер: 8-495…
>
> **I know:** on a Russian card the surname comes first, and **визи́тка** is not **ка́рта**.
