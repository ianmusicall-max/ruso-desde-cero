> **Today** you learn to count to ten, which is what you need for a phone number, a building number or a platform. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - say the numbers from 1 to 10;
> - give your phone number digit by digit;
> - read **пять** and **шесть** with their soft consonant.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

Say where each thing is, aloud:

> апте́ка (спра́ва) · банк (сле́ва) · музе́й (в це́нтре) · метро́ (далеко́)

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

**The soft т at the end.** In **пять**, **шесть**, **де́вять** and
**де́сять** the final т is soft: you finish as if a y were about to come and
never does.

> пять [pyat'] · шесть [shest'] · де́вять [DYE-vit'] · де́сять [DYE-sit']

In **шесть** the ш is hard, as always, and the е after it sounds [e]:
[shest']. And **во́семь** ends in **-мь**, with a soft м: [VO-sim'].

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat, counting on your fingers:*

> оди́н — два — три — четы́ре — пять
> шесть — семь — во́семь — де́вять — де́сять

## 3. New (8–18 min)

| Number | Russian | Say it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | оди́н | a-**DEEN** |
| 2 | два | dva |
| 3 | три | tree |
| 4 | четы́ре | chi-**TY**-ri |
| 5 | пять | pyat' |
| 6 | шесть | shest' |
| 7 | семь | syem' |
| 8 | во́семь | **VO**-sim' |
| 9 | де́вять | **DYE**-vit' |
| 10 | де́сять | **DYE**-sit' |

And zero, which you need for phone numbers: **ноль** [nol'].

**A phone number is said digit by digit**, the way English does it:

> — Ваш телефо́н?
> *Your phone number?*
> — Два — три — пять — оди́н — ноль — семь.
> *Two, three, five, one, zero, seven.*
> — Повтори́те, пожа́луйста!
> *Could you repeat that, please?*

**Counting things, not yet.** "Two coffees" or "three books" changes the
ending of the word that follows, and that comes in A2. For now, numbers on
their own: to count, point and say the number.

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**Only two look familiar.** **Три** is "three" and **два** is a distant
cousin of "two" and "duo"; the rest have to be learnt from scratch. What
English does give you is the hardest sound in the list, and you already have
it:

| Number | You'll be tempted to say | It's said |
|---|---|---|
| четы́ре | ~~[chi-TEE-ri]~~ | [chi-**TY**-ri] |
| во́семь | ~~[VO-sem]~~ | [**VO**-sim'] |
| пять | ~~[pyat]~~ | [pyat'], with a soft т |

**And the ы of четы́ре isn't an ee.** It's the one from unit 1: the i with
your tongue pulled back, as in **ты** and **мы**.

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud, forwards and backwards.*

> оди́н, два, три, четы́ре, пять, шесть, семь, во́семь, де́вять, де́сять
> де́сять, де́вять, во́семь, семь, шесть, пять, четы́ре, три, два, оди́н

### Б. Напиши́те ци́фрой.

*Write the number as a figure.*

1. семь
2. четы́ре
3. де́сять
4. два
5. во́семь
6. пять

### В. Прочита́йте телефо́н.

*Read each phone number aloud, digit by digit.*

1. 495 10 27
2. 812 39 06
3. 903 54 81

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Your numbers.** Say aloud, in Russian and digit by digit:

- your phone number;
- your building number and your flat number;
- how old two people in your family are (just the number: the whole sentence
  comes in unit 7).

Record yourself and listen: do **пять** and **шесть** end with that soft т,
or do they come out as English [pyat] and [shest]?

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> оди́н, два, три, четы́ре, пять, шесть, семь, во́семь, де́вять, де́сять · ноль · Мой телефо́н: два — три — пять…
>
> **I know:** a phone number goes digit by digit; and counting things —"two coffees"— comes in A2.
