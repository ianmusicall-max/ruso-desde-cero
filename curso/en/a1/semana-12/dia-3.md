> **Today** you finish the numbers up to one hundred, the same way as yesterday. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - say the six numbers from пятьдеся́т to сто;
> - build any number from 50 to 99;
> - read a two-digit figure without hesitating.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

From yesterday, from memory:

> два́дцать, три́дцать, со́рок, два́дцать семь, три́дцать оди́н, со́рок два.

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

Four of today's six words share the ending `-десят`, which sounds [di-siat]:

| Word | It sounds like |
|---|---|
| пятьдеся́т | pit'-di-**SIAT** |
| шестьдеся́т | shyst'-di-**SIAT** |
| се́мьдесят | **SIEM'**-di-siat |
| во́семьдесят | **VO**-sim-di-siat |

Notice: in **пятьдеся́т** and **шестьдеся́т** the stress falls on the
last syllable; in **се́мьдесят** and **во́семьдесят**, on the first.
There's no rule for it —you learn it word by word.

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat:*

> пятьдеся́т — шестьдеся́т — се́мьдесят
> во́семьдесят — девяно́сто — сто

## 3. New (8–18 min)

**The six numbers that were missing:**

| Word | It sounds like | It means |
|---|---|---|
| пятьдеся́т | pit'-di-**SIAT** | fifty |
| шестьдеся́т | shyst'-di-**SIAT** | sixty |
| се́мьдесят | **SIEM'**-di-siat | seventy |
| во́семьдесят | **VO**-sim-di-siat | eighty |
| девяно́сто | di-vi-**NOS**-ta | ninety |
| сто | sto | one hundred |

The same pattern as yesterday: tens, then units, one after the other:

> 56 → пятьдеся́т шесть
> 73 → се́мьдесят три
> 91 → девяно́сто оди́н

**Сто** is the only one of the six that doesn't follow the `-десят` pattern of the other five, and it doesn't take a unit after it either: for 101 you need a different construction, which comes later.

> — Ско́лько сто́ит биле́т?
> *How much does the ticket cost?*
> — Во́семьдесят рубле́й.
> *Eighty rubles.*

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**The pattern stays the same as yesterday's**, so today there's no new
trap: the only thing to memorize is six more words. The real risk is
mixing up tens that sound similar:

| They sound alike | They aren't the same |
|---|---|
| ше́сть (six) | шестьдеся́т (sixty) |
| се́мь (seven) | се́мьдесят (seventy) |
| во́семь (eight) | во́семьдесят (eighty) |

The unit and the tens word that starts the same way get confused by
ear: listen for whether the word stops after the first syllable or
keeps going with `-десят`.

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud, slowly the first time.*

> пятьдеся́т · шестьдеся́т · се́мьдесят · во́семьдесят · девяно́сто · сто

### Б. Как по-ру́сски?

*Say the number in Russian.*

1. 50
2. 70
3. 90
4. 56
5. 73
6. 91

### В. Семь и́ли се́мьдесят?

*Which of the two is it?*

1. 7 → а) семь · б) се́мьдесят
2. 70 → а) семь · б) се́мьдесят
3. 6 → а) шесть · б) шестьдеся́т
4. 60 → а) шесть · б) шестьдеся́т

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Read and check.** Read 56, 73 and 91 aloud, recording yourself, then
compare against the pronunciation above: can you tell the tens apart
from the units?

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **Now I can say:**
> пятьдеся́т шесть · се́мьдесят три · девяно́сто оди́н · сто
>
> **Now I know:** the six numbers from fifty to one hundred, and how to tell them apart from the units that sound similar.
