> **Today** unit 3 begins, the one about really introducing yourself, and you learn to greet people by the time of day: **до́брое у́тро**, **до́брый день**, **до́брый ве́чер**. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - greet people at any time, with **ты** and with **вы**;
> - ask someone's name politely: **Как вас зову́т?**;
> - choose the right hello for each person, which English never makes you do.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

Say hello aloud to three people: a friend, your teacher and a child.

> Приве́т! Как дела́? · Здра́вствуйте! Как вы? · Приве́т, Ми́ша!

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

**Letters that aren't said, again.** Two words you say every day are written
longer than they're said:

> здра́вствуйте [ZDRAST-vooy-tye] · пожа́луйста [pa-ZHAL-sta]

In **здра́вствуйте** the first в isn't said. In **пожа́луйста**, the у and
the й almost disappear. Say them slowly, then fast: fast, they get shorter
still.

**And the vowels that weaken.** In today's greetings, the unstressed о's at
the end sound [a]:

> до́брое у́тро [DO-bra-ye OO-tra] · до́брый день [DO-bry dyen'] · до́брый ве́чер [DO-bry VYE-chir]

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat, with the beat on the first syllable of **до́брое** and
**у́тро**:*

> До́брое у́тро! — До́брый день! — До́брый ве́чер!

## 3. New (8–18 min)

One greeting for each time of day:

| When | Greeting |
|---|---|
| in the morning | **До́брое у́тро!** |
| during the day | **До́брый день!** |
| from early evening | **До́брый ве́чер!** |
| going to bed | **Споко́йной но́чи!** (you know this one) |

**До́брый день** and **до́брый ве́чер** are polite: they suit a stranger, a
shop or work. **Здра́вствуйте** works at any time of day. And for someone you
call **ты**, there's **приве́т**, or **здра́вствуй**, without the -те at the
end.

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| до́брое у́тро | **DO**-bra-ye **OO**-tra | good morning |
| до́брый день | **DO**-bry dyen' | good afternoon, hello (during the day) |
| до́брый ве́чер | **DO**-bry **VYE**-chir | good evening |
| Здра́вствуй! | **ZDRAST**-vooy | Hello! (to someone you call ты) |
| Как вас зову́т? | kak vas za-**VOOT** | What's your name? (polite) |
| Добро́ пожа́ловать! | da-**BRO** pa-**ZHA**-la-vat' | Welcome! |

**Как вас зову́т?** is the usual question with **вас** instead of
**тебя́**: just like **А у вас?** instead of **А у тебя́?**

> — До́брый ве́чер! Добро́ пожа́ловать!
> *Good evening! Welcome!*
> — До́брый ве́чер!
> *Good evening!*
> — Как вас зову́т?
> *What's your name?*
> — Меня́ зову́т Ками́ла.
> *My name's Camila.*

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**Here English helps: "good night" is only for leaving.** Like English,
Russian greets you in the evening with **до́брый ве́чер** and keeps
**споко́йной но́чи** for going to bed. And **до́брый день**, old-fashioned
as "good day" sounds in English, is the everyday polite hello in any shop or
office.

**What English doesn't prepare you for is choosing.** "Hi" and "What's your
name?" work with anyone; Russian splits both:

| You mean | To someone you call ты | To someone you call вы |
|---|---|---|
| Hello! | Приве́т! Здра́вствуй! | Здра́вствуйте! До́брый день! |
| What's your name? | Как тебя́ зову́т? | Как вас зову́т? |

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud, with the final о's weakened.*

> До́брое у́тро! · До́брый день! · До́брый ве́чер! · Споко́йной но́чи! · Добро́ пожа́ловать!

### Б. Как поздоро́ваться?

*How do you greet them?*

1. Early in the morning, your neighbour in the lift.
2. At midday, walking into a pharmacy.
3. In the evening, arriving at a dinner.
4. At eleven at night, going off to bed.
5. A child, at any time.

### В. Тебя́ и́ли вас?

***Как тебя́ зову́т?** or **Как вас зову́т?***

1. A child in the park.
2. An elderly lady in the entrance hall.
3. The receptionist at a hotel.
4. A friend of your friend, at a student party.

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**The hotel reception.** You're the receptionist: greet by the time of day,
welcome the guest and ask their name. Then answer yourself. Record it twice,
once in the morning and once in the evening:

> — До́брое у́тро! Добро́ пожа́ловать! Как вас зову́т? — Меня́ зову́т…

Listen: do you say **здра́вствуйте** with a single в? Do the final о's of
**до́брое у́тро** sound [a]?

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> До́брое у́тро! · До́брый день! · До́брый ве́чер! · Здра́вствуй! · Как вас зову́т? · Добро́ пожа́ловать!
>
> **I know:** a greeting depends on the clock and on the person; **до́брый день** is the polite everyday hello.

Tomorrow: **Отку́да вы?** Where you're from.
