> **Today** you learn to ask and answer with the same words: the only thing that changes is your voice. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - read **Э** and **Д**;
> - say what something is or who someone is with **Э́то…**;
> - ask a yes/no question with intonation alone, and answer **Да** or **Нет**.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

Say yesterday's introduction aloud, from memory, both voices:

> — Приве́т! Как тебя́ зову́т?
> — Меня́ зову́т… А тебя́?
> — … — О́чень прия́тно!

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

The last two letters of the week:

| Letter | Looks like | Sounds | As in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Э э | a backwards E | [e] | b**e**d, softening nothing |
| Д д | — | [d] | **d**og, tongue on the teeth |

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat:*

> да — до — ду — ди — дэ
> э — эт — эн — эх — э́хо

**Put the tip of your tongue against the back of your top teeth** for
**д**, not on the ridge behind them as for an English *d*. It sounds a touch
sharper. And in italics *д* sometimes looks like a g: it's a d.

**Э or Е?** Both sound [e], but **е** softens the consonant before it (or
sounds [ye]) and **э** softens nothing. **Э** usually comes at the start of a
word: **э́то**, **э́хо**.

## 3. New (8–18 min)

**Э́то** is today's most useful word. It means "this is", "it's" and "these
are":

> **Э́то** кот. — *It's a cat.* / *This is a cat.*
> **Э́то** Анто́н и А́нна. — *These are Anton and Anna.*

You've known it since day 1: in the present tense Russian doesn't say "is".
When both parts are nouns, the missing "is" is written as a dash:
**Анто́н — студе́нт.** (Anton is a student.)

Now the question. **It has exactly the same words as the answer.** The only
thing that changes is your voice:

| | Written | Your voice |
|---|---|---|
| Statement (ИК-1) | Э́то кот. | level, then **falls** on the stressed syllable: Э́то ко↘т. |
| Question (ИК-3) | Э́то кот? | **jumps up** on the stressed syllable of the word you're asking about, then drops: Э́то ко↗т? |

> — Э́то кот?
> *Is it a cat?*
> — Да, э́то кот.
> *Yes, it's a cat.*
>
> — Э́то дом?
> *Is it a house?*
> — Нет, э́то рестора́н.
> *No, it's a restaurant.*

The jump goes where your doubt is. **Э́↗то кот?** asks whether **this** is
the cat (and not that one); **Э́то ко↗т?** asks whether it's a **cat** (and
not a dog).

And to ask who or what something is:

> — **Кто** э́то? — Э́то Анто́н.
> *Who's that? — It's Anton.*
> — **Что** э́то? — Э́то дива́н.
> *What's that? — It's a sofa.*

**Что** is said [shto]: one of the few words not read the way it's written.
And note: in Russian **кто** is for people **and animals**: **Кто э́то? —
Э́то кот.**

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| э́то | **EH**-ta | this is, it's, these are |
| да | da | yes |
| что | shto | what |
| дом | dom | house |
| вода́ | va-**DA** | water |
| дива́н | di-**VAN** | sofa |
| студе́нт | stoo-**DYENT** | student |
| до́ктор | **DOK**-tar | doctor |

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**No "is", no swapping.** English builds a question by moving words around:
*It is a cat → Is it a cat?* Russian moves nothing and adds nothing:

| You mean | ✗ Don't say | ✓ Say |
|---|---|---|
| It's a cat. | ~~Э́то есть кот.~~ | Э́то кот. |
| Is it a cat? | ~~Есть э́то кот?~~ | Э́то кот? |
| Anton is a student. | ~~Анто́н есть студе́нт.~~ | Анто́н — студе́нт. |

**And your voice jumps somewhere else.** An English yes/no question rises at
the very end and stays up: *is it a room↗?* In Russian the jump lands on
the stressed syllable of the word you're asking about, and everything after
it falls:

> Э́то ко́↗мна↘та?

Rise at the end, English-style, and you'll be understood, but it sounds
foreign. Practise with words that don't end on the stressed
syllable: **ко́мната**, **до́ктор**.

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read each pair twice: first as a statement, voice falling; then as a question, jumping up on the stressed syllable.*

> Э́то кот. — Э́то кот?
> Там дом. — Там дом?
> Э́то вода́. — Э́то вода́?
> Э́то ко́мната. — Э́то ко́мната?

### Б. Да и́ли нет?

*Answer with да or нет. The word in brackets tells you what it really is.*

1. Э́то метро́? (metro)
2. Э́то дом? (restaurant)
3. Э́то вода́? (juice)
4. Э́то до́ктор? (doctor)
5. Э́то кот? (cat)

### В. Кто и́ли что?

*Fill in кто or что.*

1. … э́то? — Э́то Анто́н.
2. … э́то? — Э́то дива́н.
3. … э́то? — Э́то студе́нт.
4. … э́то? — Э́то вода́.
5. … э́то? — Э́то кот.

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**The question game.** Point at things around you, ask yourself aloud and
answer. Sometimes the answer is yes, sometimes no:

> — Э́то дива́н? — Да, э́то дива́н.
> — Э́то окно́? — Нет, э́то… ка́рта!

Record yourself and listen: in the question, does your voice jump on the
stressed syllable and drop right after? In the answer, does it fall at the
end?

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> Э́то кот? — Да, э́то кот. · Кто э́то? · Что э́то? · Нет, э́то рестора́н.
>
> **I know:** А, О, К, М, Т, С, Р, У, Х, В, Е, Н, И, Э, Д — 15 of 33 letters.
