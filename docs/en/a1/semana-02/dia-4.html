A1 · Unit 1 · Week 2 · Day 4 · Lesson

Я, Ю, Ё and Ы

Я, Ю, Ё и Ы

Draft: this lesson hasn't had its final Russian review yet. It may change.

Today you learn the vowels with a y in front, a vowel English doesn't have, and four words you'll use in almost every sentence. By the end you'll be able to:

  • read Я, Ю, Ё and Ы;
  • say я, ты, мы, вы, and know when to say ты and when вы;
  • tell мы from ми.

1Warm-up0–3 min

Offer and answer aloud, with your voice going up and down:

— Ко́фе и́ли чай? — Чай, пожа́луйста.
— Пи́цца и́ли борщ? — Борщ, пожа́луйста!

2Pronunciation3–8 min

Letter Sounds Hint
Я я [ya] yard; after a consonant it softens it
Ю ю [yu] you
Ё ё [yo] yo-yo; always stressed, which is why it carries no mark
Ы ы [ы] a deep i: see below

Слу́шайте и повторя́йте. Listen and repeat:

я — ю — ё · ня — ню — нё · мя — мю — мё
и — ы · ми — мы · би — бы · ти — ты

Ы. Say [ee] and, without moving your lips, pull your tongue back, as if you were about to say [oo]. You get a dull, throaty sound, a bit like the i of bit said from further back. It hardly ever starts a word.

The vowels come in pairs. You now have them all. The top one keeps the consonant in front hard; the bottom one softens it (or, at the start of a word, adds a y):

hard а у о э ы
soft я ю ё е и

In today's "Say it" column, y stands for ы.

3New8–18 min

Four words that go in almost every sentence:

Word Say it Means
я ya I
ты ty you (a friend)
мы my we
вы vy you (polite, or more than one)

Я Анто́н. Ты А́нна. Мы тури́сты.
I'm Anton. You're Anna. We're tourists.

(Тури́сты is the plural of тури́ст: plurals come in unit 12. And notice again: no "am", no "are".)

Ты is for one person you're close to: a friend, your family, a child. Вы is for one person you treat with respect — someone older, a stranger, a doctor — and for more than one person, always.

And five words with the new letters:

Word Say it Means
сыр syr cheese
му́зыка MOO-zy-ka music
ю́мор YOO-mar humour
я́хта YAKH-ta yacht
ёлка YOL-ka fir tree; the New Year tree

A warning: in ordinary Russian texts ё is nearly always written without its dots, like е: you'll see елка. This course always puts the dots in.

⚠️ Contrast with English

English has one you; Russian has two. Choosing is not optional: every time you speak to someone, you pick one.

You're talking to Say
a friend, your family, a child ты
someone older, a stranger, a doctor вы
two or more people, whoever they are вы

Calling a stranger ты sounds rude; calling your best friend вы sounds cold or sarcastic.

And the vowel English doesn't have. Say ы like an English ee and you change the word:

With ы With и
мы — we ми — the note mi
был — was бил — was beating

4Practice18–25 min

А. Чита́йте.

Read aloud. Exaggerate the ы: tongue pulled back.

мы — ми · был — бил · ты — вы — я
сыр — му́зыка — ю́мор — я́хта — ёлка

Б. Ты и́ли вы?

Which do you use?

  1. You're talking to a friend.
  2. You're talking to a doctor you don't know.
  3. You're talking to two friends at once.
  4. You're talking to a child.
  5. You're talking to Vera Ivanovna, the elderly neighbour.

В. Ы и́ли и?

Write the missing letter.

  1. м… (we)
  2. с…р
  3. му́з…ка
  4. т… (you, a friend)
  5. Л…ма
  6. п…цца

5Your turn25–29 min

Point and say who. Look around you, or at a photo with people in it, and point:

Э́то я. А э́то ты. Мы — тури́сты!

Then the ы test: record yourself saying мы — ми, был — бил, and listen. If they sound the same, try again with your tongue further back.

Write Я, Ю, Ё, Ы by hand, capital and small: ы is one letter, not a "ь" and an "I".

6Wrap-up29–30 min

I can now say:
я, ты, мы, вы · Я Анто́н. Ты А́нна. · сыр, му́зыка, ёлка

I know: 31 of 33 letters. 2 to go.
