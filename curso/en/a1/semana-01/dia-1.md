> **Today** you read your first Russian words and say your first greeting. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - recognise five letters that read just like English ones;
> - read **ма́ма**, **кот**, **кто**, **там**, **так** and **как**;
> - say hello with **Приве́т!** and ask **Кто там?**

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

Listen to the most common informal Russian greeting and say it out loud
three times:

> **Приве́т!**
> *Hi! (to friends, family, people your age)*

You don't know all of its letters yet, and you don't need to: today you
learn it by ear. In a week you'll read the whole word.

See the mark over the **е**? It shows the stressed syllable: pri-**VYET**.
Real Russian books and street signs **don't write it**; this course always
does, on every word, because in Russian you can't guess where the stress
falls — and moving it can turn one word into another.

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

The Russian alphabet has 33 letters. You can already read five of them:
same shape, same sound as in English.

| Letter | Sounds | As in |
|---|---|---|
| А а | [a] | f**a**ther |
| О о | [o] | **o**r (short, no glide) |
| К к | [k] | s**k**ate |
| М м | [m] | **m**um |
| Т т | [t] | s**t**op |

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat each syllable, slowly and then fast:*

> ма — мо — ка — ко — та — то
> ам — ом — ак — ок — ат — от

A reading trap: in italics the small **т** is written *т* and looks like a
Latin **m**. It isn't an m: it's a t.

## 3. New (8–18 min)

Those five letters already give you real words. Read each one aloud twice:

| Word | Say it | Means |
|---|---|---|
| ма́ма | **MA**-ma | mum |
| кот | kot | cat |
| кто | kto | who |
| там | tam | there |
| так | tak | so, like this |
| как | kak | how |

And a whole conversation. Someone knocks on the door:

> — **Кто там?**
> *Who is it?* (literally: "who there?")
>
> — **Ма́ма.**
> *Mum.*

Notice what **isn't** there: no "is", no "a", no "the".

- **Там кот.** = *There's a cat there.* / *The cat is over there.*
- **Кто там?** = *Who's there?*

Russian has no articles, and in the present tense it doesn't say
"am / is / are". **Кот** means "cat", "a cat" and "the cat": context
decides.

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**Keep the vowels pure.** English turns *o* into a glide: *coat* is really
[koʊt]. Russian **о** never glides — **кот** is short and clean, closer to
the *o* in *or* than to *coat*. Say it with your lips still at the end.

**No puff of air.** At the start of a word English breathes out after *k*
and *t*: hold a sheet of paper in front of your mouth and say *cat* — it
moves. Russian **к** and **т** have no puff, like the *k* in *skate* and the
*t* in *stop*. Try **кот** and **там** with the paper: it should barely
move.

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read aloud. Tap the stressed syllable of the two-syllable words.*

> так — как — там — кот — кто — ма́ма

### Б. Соедини́те.

*Match each Russian word with its translation. The options use Russian
letters: **а, б, в, г, д** are the first five letters of the alphabet (said
a, be, ve, ge, de) and you'll see them in every exercise of the course.*

1. кот
2. там
3. как
4. кто
5. ма́ма

а) who · б) mum · в) cat · г) how · д) there

### В. Переведи́те.

*Translate into English.*

1. Там кот.
2. Кто там? — Ма́ма.
3. Как так?

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Someone knocks.** Ask first, out loud, then answer as the person at the
door. Do it three times, with a different answer each time:

> — Кто там?
> — Ма́ма. / — Кот! / — Том.

Then **write by hand** the five letters, capital and small, three times
each, and below them today's six words. Writing by hand fixes the shape of
a letter far better than looking at it.

## 6. Wrap-up (29–30 min)

> **I can now say:**
> Приве́т! · Кто там? · Там ма́ма. · Там кот. · Как так?
>
> **I know:** А, О, К, М, Т — 5 of 33 letters.

Tomorrow: four letters that **look** English and lie to you.
