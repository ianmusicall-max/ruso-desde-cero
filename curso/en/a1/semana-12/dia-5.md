> **Today there are no new words: there's a new sound.** You bring together the full **у … есть** paradigm —меня́, тебя́, него́, неё, нас, вас, их— with the intonation of a long question. By the end you'll be able to:
>
> - ask "do you have…?" to anyone without hesitating over the form;
> - raise your voice in the right spot in a multi-word question;
> - follow a full inventory conversation from start to finish.

## 1. Warm-up (0–3 min)

The seven pronouns of the week, out loud:

> меня́ — тебя́ — него́ — неё — нас — вас — их

## 2. Pronunciation (3–8 min)

You already know ИК-3 in short questions ("Там метро́?"): the voice
jumps up sharply on the stressed syllable and comes right back down. In
a **long** question the same thing happens, but the jump comes much
later, almost at the very end:

> У вас есть телефо́н? — the voice rises on **-фон** and drops right away.

In a short sentence the rise has barely any room; in a long one, the
ear has time to notice it.

**Слу́шайте и повторя́йте.**
*Listen and repeat, letting the voice rise where the last stress is:*

> У тебя́ есть телефо́н?
> У него́ есть су́мка?
> У вас есть расчёска, крем и нау́шники?

## 3. New (8–18 min)

No new grammar today: this dialogue uses all seven forms of the week,
one after another, in a single inventory conversation.

> — У тебя́ есть телефо́н?
> *Do you have a phone?*
> — Да, вот он. А у него́ есть су́мка?
> *Yes, here it is. And does he have a bag?*
> — Да, у него́ есть. А у неё есть де́ньги?
> *Yes, he does. And does she have money?*
> — Да, у неё есть. У нас есть часы́?
> *Yes, she does. Do we have a clock?*
> — Да, у нас есть. А у вас есть расчёска?
> *Yes, we do. And do you have a comb?*
> — Нет. А у них есть крем и нау́шники?
> *No, we don't. And do they have cream and headphones?*
> — Да, у них есть.

### ⚠️ Contrast with English

**A long English question rises near the beginning; a long Russian
question rises at the very end.** "Do YOU have a cream, a comb and
headphones?" can already rise in tone on "you"; in Russian the whole
sentence stays flat until the very last stressed word, where it jumps up
sharply:

| English (rises early) | Russian (rises at the end) |
|---|---|
| Do YOU have a cream, a comb and headphones? | У вас есть крем, расчёска и нау́шни-**КИ**? |

If you raise your voice too early in Russian, the sentence sounds like a
list, not a question.

## 4. Practice (18–25 min)

### А. Чита́йте.

*Read the dialogue from "New" aloud, raising your voice only at the very
end of each question.*

### Б. Как по-ру́сски?

*Fill in the correct form.*

1. Does he have a phone? → У ___ есть телефо́н?
2. Do we have money? → У ___ есть де́ньги?
3. Do they have a clock? → У ___ есть часы́?
4. Do you have a bag? → У ___ есть су́мка?

### В. Переведи́те.

*Translate into English, paying attention to where the voice rises.*

1. У вас есть телефо́н?
2. У неё есть зе́ркало?

## 5. Your turn (25–29 min)

**Record yourself.** Read the three long questions from "Pronunciation"
aloud, raising the pitch only on the last stressed syllable, then listen
back: can you hear the rise landing at the end and not before it?
